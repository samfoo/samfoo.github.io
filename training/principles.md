# Principles — the rules of the game

These are the settings the whole plan runs on: how to gauge effort, how to eat, how to
autoregulate, and how to handle travel. When in doubt, this file wins over the weekly plan.

## Effort — heart rate & RPE

Your runs already sit in a clean easy band (HR ~138–147). We'll keep that discipline and add
a small amount of hard work. Zones below are set from **your measured data**, not an age formula —
adjust if you do a proper max-HR or threshold test.

| Zone | HR (bpm) | Feel / RPE | Use |
|------|----------|-----------|-----|
| **Z1 recovery** | < 130 | very easy, nose-breathing | bike commute, warm-ups, walking |
| **Z2 easy (base)** | 135–150 | conversational, RPE 3–4 | the bulk of running — 80% of run time |
| **Z3 tempo/threshold** | 158–168 | "comfortably hard", short sentences, RPE 6–7 | tempo runs |
| **Z4 VO2/intervals** | 170+ | hard, RPE 8–9 | interval reps |

**Rule of thumb:** ~80% of your running minutes should be Z2 or easier, ~20% hard (Z3/Z4).
Don't let easy runs drift into Z3 — that "grey zone" is the most common mistake for newer runners
and it blunts both recovery and adaptation.

For lifting we use **RPE** (Rate of Perceived Exertion) / **RIR** (Reps In Reserve):

| RPE | Meaning |
|-----|---------|
| 6 | ~4 reps left in the tank |
| 7 | ~3 reps left |
| 8 | ~2 reps left |
| 9 | 1 rep left |
| 10 | nothing left — avoid in a deficit |

In a calorie deficit we **cap top sets at RPE 8**. The goal is to keep strength while losing fat,
not to grind.

## Nutrition — the #1 lever for goal #1

Fat loss is ~80% diet. Training preserves muscle and builds the engine; the deficit does the
fat loss. Keep it modest so it doesn't wreck running recovery or strength.

- **Target weight:** ~80 kg (16% BF at current ~67 kg lean mass). Track the **scale trend over
  2–3 weeks**, not day-to-day — bioimpedance body-fat % is noisy, so trust weight + how you look
  and lift more than the exact BF number.
- **Deficit:** ~300–500 kcal/day below maintenance → expect **~0.4–0.6 kg/week**. Slower is fine
  and protects muscle. If you stall for 2+ weeks, trim ~150 kcal or add a walk, don't crash it.
- **Protein:** **150–160 g/day** (~1.8–2.0 g/kg of *target* bodyweight). This is the single most
  important macro for keeping muscle in a deficit. Spread across meals.
- **Around training:** don't diet hardest on long-run or heavy-lift days — eat closer to
  maintenance those days, take the deficit on easy/rest days. Carbs around workouts are your friend.
- **The commute counts:** your daily bike commute + ~9k steps is meaningful NEAT. Keep it. It's
  free fat loss and active recovery — not "training," so it doesn't need programming.

## Autoregulation — reading the day

Check readiness before each session (Garmin makes this easy):

- **Resting HR** more than ~7 bpm above your baseline (~55), **poor sleep** (< ~6.5 h), or you
  just feel wrecked → **downgrade one tier**: cut a set, drop intensity, or swap a hard run for easy.
- **Body Battery / HRV low + heavy legs** → make a hard run easy, or a lift day a technique/
  back-off day. You lose nothing over 13 weeks by being conservative on a bad day.
- **Feeling great** → you may take a top set to RPE 8 rather than 7, or add distance to the long
  run. Don't chase this every session.

## Deloads

- **Planned:** every 4th week (weeks 4, 8) is lighter — see `plan.md`.
- **Autoregulated:** if resting HR trends up over a week, sleep tanks, or lifts feel heavier at the
  same weight two sessions running → take an unplanned easy week. Fitness is built during recovery.

## Travel & missed-day tiers

Your travel means "sometimes nothing." So the plan is **tiered** — on any day, pick the highest
tier you can actually do, guilt-free:

- **Tier A — Full:** the prescribed session (home barbell gym / planned run).
- **Tier B — Minimal:** hotel room or park. Running needs only shoes, so **runs almost always
  stay Tier A**. For lifting, the bodyweight session in `strength.md` (split squats, push-ups,
  inverted rows, single-leg RDL, core). 20–40 min.
- **Tier C — Nothing / recovery:** a walk, 10 min mobility, or genuine rest. This is a *valid*
  choice, not a failure. Sleep and steps still move goal #1.

### The re-entry rule (this is the important one)

**Never make up missed sessions.** If you travel and miss a week, you do **not** cram two weeks
into one on return. You resume the plan at the current calendar week, maybe repeating the last
completed week if you were out 7+ days. Trying to "catch up" is how people get hurt — the plan is
designed to lose almost nothing to a missed week.

### Priority order when a week gets compressed

If you can only do some of a week, do them in this order (matches the goal priority):

1. **One full-body lift** (protects muscle in the deficit — the thing you can't get from running).
2. **One long/quality run** (protects the aerobic engine).
3. A second easy run.
4. A second lift.
5. Climbing / extras.

Hit #1 and #2 and the week was a success, whatever else happened.
